This repository contains my winning submission to the DuQuantum 2026 Hackathon. Together with my good friend Omkar, we worked on the Alice and Bob challenge.

Given 24 hours, we were tasked with understanding how Alice and Bob uses the coherent state $|\alpha\rangle$ as its qubit. This required us to understand various concepts like open quantum systems, the Lindblad Master Equation, superconducting qubits, circuit QED, rotating wave approximation, and the interaction picture. We demonstrated our understanding of the bit flip and phase flip error profile of cat qubits, of how to perform optimal readout on cat qubits, and of how the complicated circuit Hamiltonian in 2.4 gives rise to all the dynamics we saw in the previous tasks. 

The difficulty of this challenge was to understand lots of new, complex ideas in a short span of time. The breadth of concepts spanned quantum physics, quantum information, AMO physics, mathematics, condensed matter, and superconducting qubits. 

According to judges, our project stood out because we had the best physics. This was an intentional approach on our end, and the only approach I knew how to do well. Rather than come up with creative side projects like video games and animations, we focused all our attention to answering the questions posed by Wes and coming up with the mathematical justification and physics intuition behind each answer.

Task 2.4 was the hardest of the 4 tasks, and it took us the longest to figure out. I called this "problem 6" in honor of the infamous problem 6 on the IMO. 

The .md file contains our challenge questions.

main.ipynb contains main our code and write-up submission.

appendix.ipynb contains some post-competition remarks and derivations. Given the 24 hour time constraint, some of my derivations were not fully correct/rigorous. The appendix addresses some holes in my derivations.
